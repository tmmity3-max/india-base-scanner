How the screens work

Trend Template
Price above the 50, 150 and 200-day averages in that order, the 200-day rising for a month, at least 25% above the 52-week low, within 25% of the 52-week high, and RS rating of 70 or more.
VCP
Passes the Trend Template, then at least two pullbacks in the last six months, each no deeper than the one before, the last under 12%, volume drying up, and price within 6% of the pivot (the high of the last pullback).
Blue Sky
Within 3% of the all-time high, with at least three years of history and RS of 70 or more. No old buyers waiting to sell above.
Murphy
John Murphy's positional trend-following. Weekly filter from completed weeks: price above a rising 40-week SMA. Then, within the last 5 sessions, a 10-day SMA crossing above the 50-day, a close above the last 4 full weeks' high, or a crossover confirmed by a 2-week breakout.
Base Breakout
A base starts at a swing high, the pivot. It counts once price has spent at least 3 weeks below that high with a pullback of 5% to 35%. A close above the pivot, while above the 50 and 200-day averages, is the breakout. Bases are numbered B1, B2 and so on, and the count restarts when a base undercuts the low of the one before. Listed: stocks with an RS rating of 65 or more, in one of four stages. Forming: a new base is in place and price is above its 50-day EMA; a stock with a new base is always listed here, not under its older breakout. Fresh breakout: closed above the pivot in the last 5 sessions, still above it and no more than 7% beyond. Climbing: broke out up to 60 sessions ago and has not closed back under the pivot. Played out: broke out in that time but the breakout failed, meaning price has closed back under the pivot, whether straight away or weeks later. On the chart each base is a shaded box with its pivot along the top and a green triangle on the breakout bar. Tags: Sky (pivot is an all-time high), VCP (the last third of the base is tight on lower volume), Squat (the breakout bar closed in its lower 40%).
Ichimoku
Adaptive Ichimoku. The Tenkan, Kijun and Senkou B lengths (9 / 26 / 52) shorten by up to 40% when volatility is high and lengthen when it is low. A stock is listed when a bullish signal fired in the last 5 sessions and nothing bearish has fired since. The signals: a Tenkan cross above Kijun that passes the cloud-thickness, volume and Chikou checks with a confluence score of 50 or more; a close up through the cloud on volume at least 1.3 times its 20-day average; or a retest of the cloud edge within 10 sessions of a breakout. The chart also marks cloud twists, momentum divergences and flat-Kijun support and resistance zones, and draws the setup the last signal opened: entry, a stop under the Kijun and targets at 1.5, 2.5 and 4 times the risk. The rules follow the Adaptive Ichimoku Nexus indicator by WillyAlgoTrader at its default settings.
50 EMA Pullback
A leader resting on its 50-day EMA. RS rating of 80 or more; close above the 200-day average, with the 50 EMA above it and rising; ran at least 8% above the EMA at some point in the last 40 sessions; and now closes between 2% under and 3% over the EMA. Stages: At the 50 EMA (touched it in the last three sessions, or within 1%), Bouncing (touched it in the last five sessions and closed above the previous day's high), Approaching (within 3% and no touch yet).
Episodic Pivot
After Pradeep Bonde's EP: new information makes the market reprice a stock fast. Listed: in the last month (21 sessions) the stock gapped up at least 3% and held it, or gained at least 4% and twice its usual daily range, on at least 3 times its 50-day average volume and ₹5 crore of turnover, closing in the upper half of the day. It must still be above that day's low. Catalyst says Earnings when Yahoo shows results on or just before the EP day, otherwise News / volume: the abnormal volume itself is the clue, so check the news. "Neglected before" means the stock had gone nowhere in the three months before. The chart marks the EP day's low, the usual stop.
IPO Base
Listed within the last two years, has built a base at least 10 days long and no deeper than 35%, and is now within 8% of its post-listing high.
RS rating
Weighted 12-month return, with the latest quarter counting double, ranked 1–99 against every liquid stock scanned.
Liquidity and size
Every screen only lists stocks priced at ₹50 or more, with an average daily turnover of ₹1 crore or more over the last 50 sessions and a market cap of ₹500 crore or more. The market cap buttons above the table raise that to ₹1,000 or ₹2,000 crore. Market cap comes from Yahoo Finance; a stock it has no figure for is left out.

Risk advisory

The screens and the TradingView indicator only point out stocks whose charts match a set of rules. They don't tell you where to exit. Before you act on any of them:

  1. Take a proper stop-loss in every trade. Decide where you are wrong before you buy, and place the stop-loss as soon as you enter.
  2. Manage your risk properly. Risk only a small, fixed part of your capital on any one trade, so a string of losses can't hurt you badly.
  3. Size the position from the stop. Work out the quantity from the distance between your entry and your stop-loss, not from how sure you feel.
  4. Never widen a stop-loss. Move it up to protect profit if you like, but never lower it to avoid taking a loss.
  5. Don't average down a losing trade. Adding to a position that is going against you increases the risk you already decided to take.
  6. A screen is a starting point, not a signal to buy. Check the chart, the news and the company yourself before every trade.

Not investment advice. Every list here is the output of a fixed rule applied to end-of-day data from Yahoo Finance, which can be delayed or wrong. Check the chart on your broker before acting. The author is not a SEBI-registered adviser or research analyst.
