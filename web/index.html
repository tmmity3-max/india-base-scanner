How the screens work

Trend Template
Price above the 50, 150 and 200-day averages in that order, the 200-day rising for a month, at least 25% above the 52-week low, within 25% of the 52-week high, and RS rating of 70 or more.
VCP
Passes the Trend Template, then at least two pullbacks in the last six months, each no deeper than the one before, the last under 12%, volume drying up, and price within 6% of the pivot (the high of the last pullback).
Blue Sky
Within 3% of the all-time high, with at least three years of history and RS of 70 or more. No old buyers waiting to sell above.
Murphy
John Murphy's positional trend-following. Weekly filter from completed weeks: price above a rising 40-week SMA. Then, within the last 5 sessions, a 10-day SMA crossing above the 50-day, a close above the last 4 full weeks' high, or a crossover confirmed by a 2-week breakout.
Multi-Year Base
The highest price was set two or more years ago and has not been beaten since. Price is now near or just above that old high.
IPO Base
Listed within the last two years, has built a base at least 10 days long and no deeper than 35%, and is now within 8% of its post-listing high.
RS rating
Weighted 12-month return, with the latest quarter counting double, ranked 1–99 against every liquid stock scanned.
Liquidity
Every screen only lists stocks priced at ₹50 or more with an average daily turnover of ₹1 crore or more over the last 50 sessions.

Risk advisory

The screens and the TradingView indicator only point out stocks whose charts match a set of rules. They don't tell you where to exit. Before you act on any of them:

  1. Take a proper stop-loss in every trade. Decide where you are wrong before you buy, and place the stop-loss as soon as you enter.
  2. Manage your risk properly. Risk only a small, fixed part of your capital on any one trade, so a string of losses can't hurt you badly.
  3. Size the position from the stop. Work out the quantity from the distance between your entry and your stop-loss, not from how sure you feel.
  4. Never widen a stop-loss. Move it up to protect profit if you like, but never lower it to avoid taking a loss.
  5. Don't average down a losing trade. Adding to a position that is going against you increases the risk you already decided to take.
  6. A screen is a starting point, not a signal to buy. Check the chart, the news and the company yourself before every trade.

Not investment advice. Every list here is the output of a fixed rule applied to end-of-day data from Yahoo Finance, which can be delayed or wrong. Check the chart on your broker before acting. The author is not a SEBI-registered adviser or research analyst.
